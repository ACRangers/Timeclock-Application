Reading the Timeclock

The app puts two things side by side that have never been in the same place before: the hours ServiceTitan recorded, and the work the Tracker saw during them. Here is how to navigate it, where every number comes from, and what it is actually good for.

How do I look at one person?

Tap their row. You get their calendar with a Day / Week switch, and ‹ Team brings you back.

Day names everything that happened, placed at the minute it happened. Week shows seven columns at once, with a Sun–Sat strip underneath to choose which day’s detail you are reading.

How do I get to a specific date?

Click the date in the header. It opens a date picker, so reaching last Tuesday does not mean pressing the arrow eleven times. The arrows still step a day or a week at a time, and future dates are greyed out because there is nothing there yet.

Why is my own row not in the list?

A manager reviewing the team should not be reviewing themselves, so whoever is signed in is left out. On a personal account your own hours live under My Calendar. The shared ACRManager login has no timesheet of its own, so it has no My Calendar tab at all.

What does Refresh hours do?

Re-pulls the whole displayed week from ServiceTitan straight away instead of waiting for the next automatic sync, and tells you how many punches it found. Use it when someone has just clocked in, or has just fixed a punch, and you want to see it now.

Where the numbers come from

Nothing here is typed by hand. The app reads two systems that were already running and lines them up on one clock.

WhatComes fromHow current

Nobody clocks in here. This app cannot start, stop or edit a punch. ServiceTitan stays the system of record for hours — a wrong punch gets fixed there and appears here at the next sync.

How does each activity get recorded?

Two different ways, and the difference changes how you should read a gap.

Someone clicks it

Recorded only when a person marks it in the ACRangers Tracker.

  • Estimate Sent — clicking Sent on the Estimates page.
  • Audit and Invoice — clicking Audit on the Invoices page.
  • Warranty — adding the jobs a call was about, in My Calls. Each job counts as one.

Recorded automatically

Captured from ServiceTitan as the work happens. Nobody has to remember anything.

  • Inbound and Outbound calls
  • Job Created
  • Dispatched

A missing estimate and a missing call mean different things. The automatic four are there whether or not anyone thought about the Tracker. The three that need a click only exist if someone made it — so a quiet stretch on estimates or audits may mean the work did not happen, or may mean it happened and was never marked. Worth asking which, before reading anything into it.

Whose hours am I seeing?

The thirteen people the Tracker tags with a role — Ranger, Rangerette, Red Ranger and so on — plus Sarah Lewis, who clocks in but has no Tracker record. Change someone’s role in the Tracker and this list follows automatically.

Two absences are worth knowing about: Bradley Rogers and Ivan Rodriguez are set up in ServiceTitan as technicians, so their punches file with the field crew where this app cannot reach them.

What time zone?

Arizona, always — UTC−7, no daylight saving. An hour on the calendar is an Arizona hour, whatever your own computer thinks the time is.

How do I know the data is current?

A red banner appears at the top of the Team list if the sync has failed or has simply gone quiet. That second case is the one that matters: hours which stop updating look exactly like a team that stopped working, so the app says so rather than letting the zeros speak for themselves. When you see it, press Refresh hours.

Reading a person’s day

The day view is a 24-hour calendar. Down the left runs a coloured spine — that is the clocked shift. Beside it, every tracked action sits in the ten-minute slot it happened in, with everything from the same ten minutes shown side by side.

The colour key

  • blue spineClocked in — the first eight hours of the day.
  • yellow spineOvertime. The yellow is the overtime, so its length is how much.
  • greyA meal break. Drawn, but never counted toward hours or overtime.
  • red outlineActivity in an hour with no punch under it — work happening off the clock.
  • green dotThe person wrote a note on that hour.

Why are the ten-minute rows the point?

Because density is the story. Four outbound calls at 1:38, 1:40, 1:40 and 1:44 are a four-minute burst, not an hour of steady work — stacked down the hour they would look identical to work spread across it. Side by side in one row, you can see what actually happened.

What is the Day Report underneath?

The same day written out hour by hour: the tally for each hour, then every action named with its time, then any note the person left. In Week view the Sun–Sat strip picks which day it describes, and a collapsible Week summary at the top gives you the whole week’s hours, overtime, points and totals in one place.

An empty hour is not an idle hour. The Tracker sees calls, estimates, dispatches, invoices and audits. It does not see meetings, training, one long difficult call, coaching someone at the next desk, or work done directly in ServiceTitan. A blank hour is a question, not an answer.

Points

Every tracked action carries a weight, and points appear on each hour, each day, each week, and each row of the team list. They are a total, not a ranking — the list stays in alphabetical order and nobody is coloured for a low score.

Estimate 6 Dispatched 3 Inbound 2 Job created 2 Audit 2 Invoice 2 Warranty 1.75 Outbound 0.5

What the weights are really saying

A weight is an instruction. At 6 against 0.5, one estimate is worth twelve outbound calls — so a day of steady phone work will score below a day of estimates, by design. That is the sharpest ratio in the set, and the first number to revisit if the scoreboard starts pulling people somewhere you did not intend.

Read points as what kind of work this was, not how hard someone tried. Two people on the same total may have had completely different days.

What to use it for

The morning coverage check

Open the Team list on today. Who is on, who is off, who started late. Thirteen rows, about five seconds — this is the thing the app is fastest at.

Catching missed clock-outs before payroll

A red dot on a past day means someone never clocked out. Left alone it becomes a wrong number on a timesheet. Step back through the week and clear them while people still remember the day; the fix happens in ServiceTitan and shows up here at the next sync.

Seeing overtime while you can still act on it

Overtime appears the moment it happens, not at the end of the pay period. Mid-week you can still move work around; on Friday afternoon you cannot. Open Week view and the summary carries the running total per person.

Finding work that happens off the clock

Red-outlined hours are activity with no punch underneath — someone answering calls before they clock in, or after they clock out. That usually means unpaid time rather than stolen time. Worth a conversation either way: either the punches need to move, or someone is quietly working for free.

Preparing a one-to-one

Open the person, switch to Week, expand the summary. You have their hours, their overtime, what they actually did each day, and anything they wrote about it — instead of an impression. The specifics are the useful part: Tuesday you sent nine estimates lands very differently from you seem busy lately.

Asking about a quiet stretch, well

If an afternoon looks empty, the app has told you the Tracker saw nothing. It has not told you that nothing happened. Ask what they were working on — often the answer is a meeting, a training session, or one call that ran an hour. If they add it as a note, the record is better next time.

What it will not do

  • Approve time. There is no approval step yet. This is for reviewing, not signing off.
  • Export. No CSV, no download. Payroll still comes out of ServiceTitan.
  • Edit hours. Read-only by design; punch corrections happen in ServiceTitan.
  • Let you write someone’s note. Notes belong to the person whose hour it is. You can read them, not author them.
  • Show the whole team’s week at once. Week view is one person at a time.
  • Cover field technicians. Different work, different data — deliberately out of scope.
